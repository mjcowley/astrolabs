The C5 proforma

Overview

In this AstroLab you'll hunt for an exoplanet using the transit method. In Part 1, you'll explore the giant planet WASP-2b in a professional exoplanet archive, track down its star in ten images from a telescope on the ground, and use AstroImageJ to measure the tiny dip in its brightness as the planet passes in front of it. In Part 2, you'll compare your light curve with the same planet seen by NASA's TESS space telescope, work out what kind of world it is, and see how measurements like yours reveal a planet's size, orbit and temperature.

By the end of this AstroLab you'll be able to:

  • explain how the transit method finds exoplanets, and why it's better at finding some kinds of planets and planetary systems than others
  • look up an exoplanet in a professional archive, and find a star in your own images using an online sky atlas
  • choose reference stars and an aperture size, and use differential photometry in AstroImageJ to build a light curve
  • read a transit's depth, duration and period from a light curve, and explain what each tells us about a planet
  • explain why space telescopes and many measurements are needed to find small planets

How to complete this AstroLab

You can complete C5 on campus in your scheduled session, or remotely in your own time. The steps are the same either way, and everyone submits the same proforma.

On campus

  • Come to your scheduled AstroLab session. AstroImageJ is already installed on the lab computers.
  • The teaching team will run a background information session and help you as you go.

Remote

  • Watch the recording of the background information session on Echo360. It covers everything from the live session.
  • Install AstroImageJ on your own computer, or use QUT's Virtual Desktop Infrastructure.
  • Work through Before you start on the GitHub page first, so setup problems don't hold you up.
  • If you get stuck, contact the teaching team.

Finished? Submit your completed proforma on the Assignments page.
